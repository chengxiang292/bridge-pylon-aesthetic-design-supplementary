# Survey screening and analysis cohort

The English workbook preserves all 56 collected responses. The main manuscript analysis uses a 50-participant subset, with original participant IDs preserved. This file documents the previously applied screening and does not introduce a new selection.

## Operational rules

The screening was developed post hoc. It did not use whether the participant preferred SIMP, two-stage TO, or RL refinement.

1. **R1:** At least four of the ten cases have a rating–preference inconsistency.
2. **R2:** All 30 relative ratings are identical across the ten cases and three alternatives.
3. **R3:** Completion time is below 90 seconds and at least two cases have a rating–preference inconsistency.

An inconsistency is flagged when at least one preferred alternative has a lower relative rating than another alternative in that case. For a pairwise preference, both selected alternatives must attain the highest rating to avoid a flag. A no-difference response is flagged if the three relative ratings are unequal. These comparisons are screening diagnostics, not proof that either answer is invalid.

## Limitations

Uniform ratings may reflect genuine indifference, and rating and preference questions need not produce identical distinctions. In particular, participant 17 differentiates among alternatives in the preference questions despite uniform relative ratings. The 90-second threshold is an exploratory operational choice and was not independently validated. The omitted responses should therefore not all be described as proven random or invalid responses.

The earlier sensitivity analysis additionally considered a broader 52-response subset, omitting only IDs 8, 11, 28, and 47, and the complete 56-response set. The workbook in this repository preserves all 56 responses so that alternative screening decisions remain possible.

The high-rating rate, exclusive-best rate, and best-including-pairwise-ties rate are distinct outcomes. ND responses remain in the denominator and count toward no method in the preference numerators. A pairwise tie counts toward both selected methods, so the rates including pairwise ties may sum to more than 100% across methods.
